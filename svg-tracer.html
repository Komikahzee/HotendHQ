How to convert an image to SVG

  1. Add your image. Choose a PNG, JPG or WebP, drop it on the page or paste it. Use the biggest version you have.
  2. Pick a mode. Colour for logos, decals and cartoons (switch on Remove shading for flat colours), Black & white for stencils and cut files, Detailed for photos and shaded artwork.
  3. Check it. Drag the compare line, zoom in, and adjust colours, smoothness and specks until it looks right.
  4. Download. Save the SVG, copy the code, or download one SVG per colour for cutters and multicolour 3D prints.

Built for artwork

Logos, decals, emblems, stickers and line art come out as smooth curves with sharp corners kept sharp. Anti-aliased and JPEG edges are cleaned up first, so you get no coloured fringes or slivers.

No gaps between colours

Each edge between two colours is traced once and shared by both shapes, so they always meet exactly. Pick separate pieces for vinyl cutters, laser cutters and multicolour 3D prints.

Private and free

The tracing runs on your own device. Your image is never uploaded, and there is no sign-up, watermark or limit.

Tips for the cleanest trace

Which images trace best?

Any image works, as long as you pick the right mode. Logos, decals, badges, stickers, icons and cartoons work best in Colour mode. Photos, renders and shaded artwork belong in Detailed mode. The bigger and sharper the image, the more detail survives, so use the largest version you have.

How do I remove the shading and get flat colours?

In Colour mode, switch on Remove shading. Each area that is shaded from light to dark becomes one solid colour, while real outlines stay where they are. Use Shading removal to choose how strongly it flattens: Strong for cartoons and logos, Light for photos.

Can I convert a photo to SVG?

Yes. Use Detailed mode. It splits the photo into thousands of shapes that follow its edges and fills shaded ones with real SVG gradients, so the result looks like the photo but scales like a vector. Files are bigger than a logo's, so lower the level of detail if you need a smaller file.

A small colour went missing. How do I get it back?

Use Pick a missing colour and click that colour on the image. It is added to the palette and the image is traced again. You can also raise Colour detail, or switch off automatic colours and set the number yourself.

How do I change or remove a colour?

Click a swatch to give that colour a new value in the SVG. The eye button hides a colour so it is left out of the file, and the cross removes it so its areas are taken over by the colours around them.

Stacked or separate pieces?

Stacked fills holes under the shapes on top, which hides the hairline seams some viewers show between touching shapes. Separate pieces never overlap, which is what cutting machines and multicolour 3D printing need. Use Layers to download one SVG per colour.

How do I make a 3D print from the SVG?

Most slicers, including Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer and PrusaSlicer, can import an SVG and extrude it. For a layered sign or a stencil, our layered sign and stencil generators take an image directly.

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. The whole trace happens in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server.

Is it really free?

Yes. There is no sign-up, no watermark and no limit on how many images you convert.

Which file types can I convert?

PNG, JPG, WebP, GIF, BMP and AVIF images. The result is a standard SVG that works in Cricut Design Space, Silhouette Studio, Inkscape, Illustrator, Figma, LightBurn and 3D printing slicers. For cutting machines and LightBurn, use Colour or Black & white mode with separate pieces.
